Water Damage We See Most in San Francisco

Every house has a few weak points where water tends to get loose. These are the ones San Francisco property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. Heaters in a hallway closet or attic can ruin flooring and ceilings well beyond the utility area.

Roofs: Wind can lift shingles and drive rain underneath them in a single storm. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. For roof leaks in San Francisco, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. The first to go are hose bibs, irrigation lines and any pipe routed through an unheated space. A house left empty or a wing kept unheated faces the greatest risk. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: Gurgling sounds and more than one slow fixture at once suggest the main drain is partly blocked. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. We remove any standing water in San Francisco homes, dry the walls and slab, and explain what likely caused it.

Simple Ways to Keep a San Francisco Home Dry

A flood in the house feels like it came from nowhere, yet most trace back to wear that built up over months or years. Knowing the usual suspects is most of the prevention for a San Francisco homeowner.

Plumbing comes first. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Appliances are second. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. Third is the outside of the building. Missing shingles, cracked flashing, blocked gutters and soil that slopes toward the house all let rain find a way in.

Going away for more than a few days? Close the main valve before you go, or have a neighbor look in. Inglewood Restoration is glad to talk through prevention with any homeowner in San Francisco. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.
